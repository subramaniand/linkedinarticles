Question answering gives you an answer. Conversational language understanding gives you an intent. That difference decides where your business logic lives.

Both are learned features that need labelling, training and deployment. What they return is very different.

Question answering matches a question to a knowledge base and returns a fixed answer your app simply displays. You can seed it from existing FAQ pages, documents and prebuilt chit-chat pairs, then add follow-up prompts for multi-turn exchanges.

Conversational language understanding returns a label: the most likely intent plus entities such as dates, quantities or phone numbers. Nothing happens until your code acts on it.

Getting this wrong hurts. Build an intent model for what is really an FAQ, and you maintain handlers just to print text. Push "do something" requests into a knowledge base, and it can only reply with words.

It also changes ownership. Content editors can own a knowledge base. Developers own intent handlers.

A simple test: sort expected requests into "tell me" and "do this for me". Route the first to question answering, the second to intent handling. Need both? Add a router.
